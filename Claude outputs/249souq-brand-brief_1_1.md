- Zero-inventory marketplace — 249Souq never holds stock

## 2. Brand Identity

**Personality:** Warm, community-first, culturally authentic, nostalgic, proud — **never corporate, never generic "startup."** Should feel like it was made by and for Sudanese people, not a faceless e-commerce brand.

**Emotional core:** Home, belonging, nostalgia, cultural pride, the sensory memory of Sudan (scent, texture, ritual) carried by people who live far from it.

**Language:** Fully bilingual, Arabic-first. Arabic copy uses warm, colloquial Sudanese-accented Arabic (not formal MSA) where natural — phrases like "بكرة"، "ريحة البيت"، "حاجة حلوة جاية". English copy is used for GCC-wide/international diaspora reach but Arabic carries the emotional weight. **Exception: formal documents (contracts, agreements) use proper Modern Standard Arabic throughout, never mixed with colloquialisms.**

**⚠️ Seating convention — IMPORTANT correction (added 15 September 2026):** In real Sudanese homes, people do NOT sit cross-legged directly on the floor/rug to work, eat, or prepare things. The correct depiction is either: (a) seated properly on the sala/majlis-style sofa or floor-level cushioned seating, working at the low carved wooden coffee table placed in front, with the tray/plate resting ON the table, not on the floor or in the lap; or (b) seated at a regular dining table. Any image prompt involving a person "sitting" for a task must explicitly specify one of these two setups and explicitly exclude floor-seated/cross-legged-on-the-ground poses.

**⚠️ Doorstep shoes/shibshib — correction (added 16 September 2026):** Do not include shoes or slippers in doorway/entrance shots at all unless specifically needed for the scene — leave the entryway floor clean and uncluttered. If shoes must be included, explicitly specify they face outward/away from the door as if just stepped out of.

**⚠️ Greeting gestures — correction (added 16 September 2026):** For any scene depicting people greeting each other, specify an actual traditional Sudanese greeting explicitly — typically a warm handshake (right hands clasped), often with heads inclined toward each other — rather than leaving the gesture to the model's interpretation.

**⚠️ Coffee vessel — correction (added 16 September 2026):** The correct vessel is the **jabana sudaniya (جبنة سودانية)** — a traditional black clay coffee pot with a rounded body and long spout — served in small clay or simple ceramic cups, not ornate floral porcelain or a brass/metal Gulf-style dallah. Any coffee-serving prompt must explicitly specify "traditional Sudanese black clay jabana pot (جبنة سودانية), not a brass or metal dallah."

**⚠️ Jabana scale — correction (added 16 September 2026):** Any jabana close-up must explicitly specify it is **small-to-medium and realistically proportioned to the hand holding it** — roughly the size that comfortably fits in one hand, similar in scale to a small teapot, not an oversized decorative vessel.

**⚠️ Coffee server gender — correction (added 16 September 2026):** In the coffee-pouring close-up itself, specify **a woman's hand (with a toub or modest sleeve visible at the wrist)** pouring the jabana, not a man's hand. Traditionally coffee is prepared and served by a woman ("سيدة القهوة" / the coffee host). **See the woman-presence correction below** for whether/how she should appear in other scenes of the series.

**⚠️ Toub fabric/pattern — correction (added 16 September 2026):** A real Sudanese toub is a plain, solid-color, lightweight wrap fabric (commonly white or a soft pastel), with no colorful geometric cross-stitch embroidery border (that reads as Ethiopian/Eritrean, not Sudanese). Any prompt showing a toub or toub sleeve must explicitly specify **plain/solid-color fabric with no embroidery or cross-stitch pattern**.

**⚠️ Mabkhara finish — correction (added 16 September 2026):** The brass mabkhara (incense burner) itself is correct and authentic. Specify a **dark, aged brass/bronze finish with a pierced, perforated lid**, not a shiny, ornate gold finish — a shiny gold censer reads as generic "Arabian Nights" rather than Sudanese.

**⚠️ Woman-presence correction — REVISED (added 16 September 2026):** **Default for the hospitality series from Scene 3 (bukhoor) onward: do NOT include the woman in the background — keep the scene to just the three recurring men, seated together on the sofa.** She may still appear in the Scene 2 coffee-pouring close-up itself (hand/sleeve only), but should not be shown standing over or serving the seated men in wider/background shots (reads as servant/hierarchical). If a future scene calls for showing her again, she must be seated as an equal participant (never standing while others sit, never in a serving posture). **See also the self-serving-host and family-roles corrections below**, which now govern who serves food in this series.

**⚠️ Group visibility — correction (added 16 September 2026):** Any prompt showing the recurring group of three men (or any small group) must explicitly specify that **all people's faces are visible and engaged with each other/the camera** — smiling, in conversation, facing generally inward toward the table or each other — never with a person's back fully turned away or only the back of their head/turban shown.

**⚠️ Serving dishes — correction, REVISED (added 16 September 2026):** Traditional Sudanese home serving dishes are typically **plain or simply-patterned enamel or tin plates, in solid colors (white, red, or blue trim/rim), not ornate ceramic with leaf, floral, or paisley motifs.** **Specify shallow, flat-profile plates/dishes (wide, low-rimmed), not deep bowls** — the food should be spread thin and visible across the plate, not mounded up in a bowl shape.

**⚠️ Plate distribution / meal layout — correction, REVISED (added 16 September 2026):** **Default going forward: do NOT specify a communal tray or a central aseeda dome at all.** Instead, use **individual plain enamel/tin serving plates** placed directly on the coffee table or dining table — no single large tray uniting them, no central dome dish. Bread should still be either a thin lace-textured kisra/flatbread or a round loaf in a woven basket beside the plates, not stacked flatbread on a plate.

**⚠️ Self-serving-host correction (added 16 September 2026):** When a scene needs to show dishes being placed/set down on the table, do NOT show a separate woman or servant-like figure standing over the seated men to place the dishes. Instead, have **one of the three recurring men lean forward from the sofa and place/adjust a dish himself**, mid-conversation, as a host serving his own guests among equals. **See the family-roles correction below for exactly which of the three men should be doing the serving.**

**⚠️ Family-roles correction (added 16 September 2026):** **The three recurring men now have fixed roles for the rest of the hospitality series:** (1) **the father** — the eldest, grey-bearded, white-turbaned man — is the head of the household; he sits and receives warmly, and should never be shown serving or placing dishes; (2) **the son** — a younger man (skullcap/kufi) — is the father's son and the one who actually serves, placing dishes on the table himself; (3) **the guest** — a distinct third man — is the one being hosted throughout the series (the one who initially declined hospitality and ends up staying); he should be shown receiving, seated attentively, never serving. **There is only ever one guest in a given scene.** Keep these three identities visually consistent (same faces/build/dress) across every scene for continuity.

**⚠️ Expanded dish list correction — REVISED (added 16 September 2026):** In addition to لحمة محمرة, foul (ful medames), and salata, the food-spread dish rotation should also draw from: **بيض مسلوق (boiled eggs)**, **طعمية (Sudanese-style taemia/falafel)**, and **شعيرية (vermicelli, sweet or savory)**. A given scene should show **5–6 individual small dishes total** when a fuller spread is called for, still using plain enamel/tin, flat/shallow-profile dishes arranged simply and not congested — never a single crowded row, and never a communal tray/aseeda. **The بيض مسلوق plate specifically should show a generous portion — 6-8 egg halves, not just 2-3.**

**⚠️ Character-consistency correction (added 16 September 2026):** Text-only prompts do not reliably keep the same faces/builds for the father, son, and guest across separate generation rounds. **Going forward, always attach 1-2 previously approved images of the three recurring men as reference images alongside any new scene prompt**, with an explicit instruction such as "keep these exact same three men — same faces, same build, same dress — do not generate new faces." Do not rely on text description alone to preserve character continuity. **This applies to any recurring-character series, including the Al Nuzahi & Yousef duo (see Section 5a) once their first approved images exist.**

**⚠️ Drinkware correction — REVISED (added 16 September 2026):** **Add a clear glass water pitcher/jug (filled with water) and 2-3 matching clear glass drinking cups placed among the food plates — but only in full meal-spread scenes** (i.e. scenes with the 5-6 food dishes from the expanded-dish-list correction). **For a coffee/bukhoor-only scene (no food dishes present), keep the table minimal: just the jabana, 2-3 coffee cups, and the mabkhara — do not add the water pitcher/glasses there.**

**⚠️ Doorstep-farewell / ونسة خشم الباب correction — REVISED (added 16 September 2026):** For the closing scene of the "hospitality" carousel series, the finalized direction is a well-known Sudanese cultural beat: **ونسة خشم الباب** — the tradition of guests who are ostensibly leaving getting pulled into an extended goodbye conversation right at the doorstep (often 10+ minutes), before actually departing. **The finalized composition: all three men — father, son, and guest — are standing OUTSIDE the house together, on the front step/threshold, with the front door open behind them** (not two men still inside the doorway); the setting must read as an authentic Sudanese home exterior — warm sandy/earth-toned plaster or mudbrick-style walls, a traditional carved wooden door, simple low boundary walls or a modest street glimpse, palm trees — not a generic Gulf-villa, Mediterranean, or unspecified Middle Eastern streetscape. The **guest** should not be carrying any bag, briefcase, or other prop unless a scene explicitly calls for it — hands are free, used only for animated mid-conversation gestures. Golden/sunset lighting, all three faces visible and engaged, no shoes/slippers at the doorstep unless naturally off to the side facing outward. This concept is also flagged as a strong reusable content idea beyond this carousel — a standalone post built around "you know you're Sudanese when the goodbye takes 20 minutes" is worth doing independently of the hospitality series.

## 7. Tone & Voice Guidelines for Any Generated Content

- Warm and personal, like a message from a friend/community member — never salesy or corporate
- Culturally specific and proud — references to Sudan, home, family, tradition should feel genuine, not decorative
- Community-first framing: 249Souq is positioned as a platform *for* the diaspora and *for* Sudanese sellers, not a profit-first retailer
- Bilingual by design — Arabic carries emotional weight, English extends reach
- Avoid generic diaspora-marketing clichés; be specific to Sudanese culture (bukhoor rituals, toub, souq imagery, specific Arabic phrasing) rather than generic "Middle Eastern" or "African" branding
