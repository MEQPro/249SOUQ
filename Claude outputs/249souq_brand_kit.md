# 249Souq.com — Brand Kit
*For use as reference context when generating images/content in ChatGPT or any AI tool.*

## 1. What this brand is
249Souq.com is the first global online marketplace for the Sudanese diaspora — starting with the UAE and GCC, expanding to Europe. It connects Sudanese small business owners (bukhoor, oud, traditional garments, food, handmade goods) with Sudanese families living abroad who miss home. Founded by Salwa Ahmed under Marka Di Fantasya General Trading LLC (UAE).

Positioning: a warm, community-run marketplace — never a cold corporate storefront. The feeling should be "your cousin's trusted seller network," not "Amazon for Sudanese products."

## 2. Taglines (use the right one for context)
- **Community tagline** (used site-wide, social bios, general brand moments): **من ناسنا لي ناسنا** — "from our people, to our people"
- **Product/taste tagline** (used specifically for food/home-comfort product content, e.g. bukhoor): **طعم البيت، وين ما مشيت** — "the taste of home, wherever you've gone"

## 3. Logo
Two approved logo forms — files attached alongside this brief:
- **Wordmark** (`logo-249souq-transparent.png`): "249" in brand green + "Souq.com" in flag red, black underline rule, community tagline beneath in black. Use on light/white backgrounds — this is the primary web/app logo.
- **Emblem/badge** (`logo-with-slogan.png`): circular gold double-ring badge on a dark brown/near-black background, "249" in cream serif numerals, "SOUQ" spaced out in gold small-caps below, taste tagline in gold script beneath the badge. Use for social profile pictures, packaging, or anywhere a badge/seal feel fits better than a horizontal wordmark.
- Do not recolor the logo, distort its proportions, or place the wordmark on a busy/dark photo background without a contrast plate.

**Palette mood**: warm cream base, earthy browns, deep maroon and gold as cultural accent colors, green as the one confident "brand" color, red used sparingly (logo + flag reference only). This should read as *warm desert-and-home*, not corporate tech, not neon, not pastel-cute.

## 5. Typography
- **Headings, logo, section titles**: Lalezar (falls back to Tajawal) — a bold, rounded, friendly Arabic-Latin display face. Used at regular weight (400) with slight letter-spacing, or at heavier weight for large section titles.
- **Body text, buttons, nav**: Tajawal — clean, modern, highly legible Arabic sans-serif, right-to-left by default. Buttons/nav are bold (weight 700-800); paragraph text is regular weight.
- Site is RTL-first (Arabic is the primary reading direction); English is used alongside, not instead of, Arabic.

## 6. Visual / photography style (what "on-brand" imagery looks like)
- Real Sudanese homes and traditional sala (majlis-style) interiors: red-and-black patterned floor cushions/sofas, carved wooden coffee tables, woven straw wall baskets, palm-tree river landscape paintings, sheer floral curtains, warm afternoon window light.
- Women in colorful floral thobes/toabs and headscarves (lavender, cream-and-floral, green, dusty rose) — warm, candid, mid-conversation or mid-task, never posed like stock photography.
- Everyday domestic rituals as the subject: brewing coffee/tea, lighting bukhoor in a brass mabkhara with visible soft smoke, welcoming guests, sitting in a circle with small coffee cups and dates.
- Product shots (bukhoor jars, oud, etc.) are gold-lidded, warm-toned, shot in natural or warm indoor light — not sterile studio-white e-commerce shots.
- Photorealistic, warm color grading (warm whites, golden light), soft natural shadows — never harsh flash, never oversaturated, never cartoon/illustration style unless explicitly requested for a specific graphic.

## 7. Tone of voice
- Warm, community-first, culturally authentic — like a trusted family friend, never a corporate brand voice.
- Bilingual by default: Arabic (Sudanese colloquial where natural, e.g. "شنو"، "ريحة"، "زول") paired with English, not translated stiffly.
- Never salesy/pushy language; lead with belonging and nostalgia, let the product/CTA follow naturally.
- Short, conversational lines over long marketing copy — captions and overlay text should read like something a person would actually say, not an ad.

## 8. What to avoid
- Sterile, corporate, "SaaS dashboard" visual language (flat icons, cold blues, minimalist tech aesthetic) — wrong category entirely for this brand.
- Generic "Middle Eastern souq" clichés that aren't specifically Sudanese (e.g. Gulf-only abayas with no Sudanese toab/thobe styling, generic Arabian Nights imagery).
- Overly staged, stock-photo-looking smiles or poses.
- Neon or pastel color schemes — stay within the warm cream/brown/gold/green/maroon palette above.
