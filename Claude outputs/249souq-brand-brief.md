## 1. Business Overview

**Name:** 249Souq.com
**Legal entity:** Marka Di Fantasya General Trading LLC (UAE trade license, active)
**Founder:** Salwa Ahmed — biomedical engineer, 15+ years experience in UAE/GCC healthcare infrastructure, PMP certified
**What it is:** The first global marketplace built specifically for the Sudanese diaspora — connecting Sudanese small business owners and artisans with Sudanese people living abroad, starting in the UAE/GCC and expanding to Europe (UK first).

**Tagline:** "طعم البيت، وين ما مشيت" (Arabic — "The taste of home, wherever you go")
**Alternate slogan used in content:** "ريحة البيت، وين ما مشيت" ("The scent of home, wherever you go" — bukhoor-specific variant)

**Business model:**
- Zero-inventory marketplace — 249Souq never holds stock
- Commission per sale: 10–15% of GMV
- Small onboarding fee from sellers (one-time or recurring)
- Logistics margin: the UAE trade license is used to book couriers (Aramex, etc.) at SME/business rates; sellers/customers are charged slightly above that rate, and 249Souq keeps the difference
- Growth path: single-product organic launch → Shopify-style multi-vendor marketplace → mobile app → GCC-wide → European diaspora (UK, etc.)

**Current phase:** Organic launch, no paid ads yet. Single product live (bukhoor), building community first via TikTok, Instagram, WhatsApp broadcast, and Facebook Groups targeting the Sudanese diaspora. Launch date: **Friday 11 September 2026**.

## 2. Brand Identity

**Personality:** Warm, community-first, culturally authentic, nostalgic, proud — **never corporate, never generic "startup."** Should feel like it was made by and for Sudanese people, not a faceless e-commerce brand.

**Emotional core:** Home, belonging, nostalgia, cultural pride, the sensory memory of Sudan (scent, texture, ritual) carried by people who live far from it.

**Language:** Fully bilingual, Arabic-first. Arabic copy uses warm, colloquial Sudanese-accented Arabic (not formal MSA) where natural — phrases like "بكرة", "ريحة البيت"، "حاجة حلوة جاية". English copy is used for GCC-wide/international diaspora reach but Arabic carries the emotional weight.

**Visual/color system (Sudanese heritage identity, implemented site-wide):**
- **Primary gold/cream palette** — warm, souq-market, lantern-lit tones (existing base palette)
- **Deep maroon accent:** `#7A1F2B` (added as heritage accent color)
- **Maroon dark:** `#4C0F1A`
- **Maroon light:** `#9C3347`
- **Brown-deep** (dark background/base tone used in hero and footer)
- Heritage motifs referenced: traditional Sudanese wooden objects (e.g., "المركوب" — carved wooden items), prayer beads, crescent moon, geometric diamond ornaments (used as divider/accent shapes site-wide)
- Typography: Tajawal (Google Font) for Arabic-friendly, warm, rounded sans-serif feel
- Iconography: clean line-icon style (Tabler Icons) — no emoji, no cartoonish icons; icons feel elevated/premium, not amateur or "AI-generated looking"
- Overall aesthetic target: **premium souq / heritage marketplace** — think warm golden-hour lighting, traditional textures (fabric, wood, brass, incense smoke), NOT flat corporate SaaS design

**What to avoid visually:** generic stock-photo diaspora imagery, overly staged/artificial AI look, cold/corporate color palettes (blues, clinical whites), Western minimalist e-commerce tropes that erase cultural specificity, emoji used as UI icons.

## 3. Target Audience

**Primary:** Sudanese diaspora, first wave: UAE and GCC (Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)
**Secondary (near-term expansion):** Sudanese communities in Europe, starting with the UK
**Demographics:** Sudanese families and individuals living abroad who miss home — spans generations (the brand imagery explicitly includes mother/daughter intergenerational moments — passing down heritage/tradition)
**Psychographic hook:** People who want a tangible, authentic connection to Sudanese culture and want to support real Sudanese small businesses/artisans, not mass-produced imports.
**Platforms where audience lives:** TikTok, Instagram, WhatsApp (broadcast lists/status), Facebook Groups (diaspora community groups specifically — not general FB audience)

**Toub (traditional Sudanese women's wrap garment)** is a recurring wardrobe element worth using in lifestyle imagery — signals authenticity immediately to the target audience.

## 7. Tone & Voice Guidelines for Any Generated Content

- Warm and personal, like a message from a friend/community member — never salesy or corporate
- Culturally specific and proud — references to Sudan, home, family, tradition should feel genuine, not decorative
- Community-first framing: 249Souq is positioned as a platform *for* the diaspora and *for* Sudanese sellers, not a profit-first retailer
- Bilingual by design — Arabic carries emotional weight, English extends reach
- Avoid generic diaspora-marketing clichés; be specific to Sudanese culture (bukhoor rituals, toub, souq imagery, specific Arabic phrasing) rather than generic "Middle Eastern" or "African" branding

## 8. Practical Notes for the Generation Platform

- All final on-site copy currently reflects **AED 80 all-in pricing** (product + delivery) — use this figure, not AED 60, in any new content generated from this point forward.
- Any generated imagery should be able to work as: (a) a website hero banner (wide/landscape, must have space for headline text overlay, needs to also crop sensibly to a narrow vertical mobile format), (b) square/vertical social posts (Instagram feed + Reels/TikTok), and (c) WhatsApp Status format (vertical, short-lived, high visual impact).
- Keep faces/skin tones consistent with a Sudanese/East African appearance when generating people — this is core to authenticity for this audience.
- Branding element to include when relevant: "249Souq" wordmark/logo, gold-lidded jar packaging, and (for delivery-themed content) a visibly branded cardboard parcel box.
